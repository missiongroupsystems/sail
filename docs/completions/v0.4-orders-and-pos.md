# v0.4 Orders and the POS: completion note

Date: 4 Nov 2026. Branch: `feat/beta-v1`. Roadmap phase 4 (v0.3 → v0.4).

## What

The register side of orders: station pairing with one-time codes and long-lived station tokens; 6-digit PIN sessions with inactivity expiry and lockout; single-use manager override grants bound to one action and target; one order model with the four order types and per-type validation; order numbers per outlet with a source prefix; lines with modifier validation (min/max, per-modifier maximum, two-level nesting), availability, sold-out and atomic daily caps; fire/hold, partial and full voids (Manager+ after sending), cancel, change type with a full re-price, move table, attach/detach customer, manual pricing-rule applications with the rule's own role, override and reason settings; the pricing pipeline run on every change with adjustments and per-line amounts persisted under database-enforced sum invariants; optimistic concurrency with clean conflicts; dry runs; the outlet event stream with catch-up; the floor snapshot and order lists. The iPad POS app is built against this contract (see the web status below).

## How

- **Every mutation ends the same way:** `repriceOrder` runs `price()` on the current non-voided lines, manual applications, the customer's groups and the active rules, persists line amounts and adjustments, then `bumpVersion` and `loadSnapshot`. Deferred constraint triggers assert at commit that each adjustment's lines sum to its total and that the order total equals the sum of its line totals.
- **Snapshots are the only way clients learn amounts.** Every order mutation returns `{ data: snapshot, events, warnings }`; the snapshot carries `totals`, per-line `amounts` and `adjustments` with per-line amounts.
- **Concurrency:** `expected_version` is checked under `SELECT … FOR UPDATE` on the order row; a mismatch is `version_conflict` with the current version. Appends without a version are allowed (an add never destroys someone else's edit), which is the "rebase where safe" behaviour the design calls for.
- **Dry runs** mark the transaction, skip sequence allocation in `appendEvent` (events come back with `seq: -1`), and the pipeline rolls back after the handler; the response carries `x-dry-run: true`.
- **Overrides:** `POST /v1/overrides` finds the approver by PIN among Manager+ staff scoped to the outlet (indexed HMAC lookup, scrypt verify), refuses self-approval, and issues a 2-minute single-use grant bound to action and target. Protected handlers consume it through `requireRoleOrOverride`, and the approver is recorded on the event and the audit entry.
- **Stock:** `consumeDailyCap` is one conditional `UPDATE … RETURNING`; a race of two stations on the last unit leaves exactly one winner (tested).
- **Extension points** for later phases: `onFire` and `onVoid` hooks (print jobs, v0.5), `registerPaidProvider` and `registerPaidLineGuard` (payments, v0.6), `registerSnapshotExtras` (checks and balances), `registerFloorExtra` (QR rounds and table requests, v0.8).

## Why

The POS is the heart of v0.9 (scope §4.3) and the first consumer of the pricing pipeline. Building the register's primitives first, with the invariants in the database, means the iPad app (and later agents over MCP) can only ever press buttons whose outcome is already verified.

## Web (POS) status

The iPad POS (`packages/web/src/app/(pos)`) is delegated UI work against this contract: lock and PIN, floor, order entry with modifier sheet, order sheet and action dock, override sheet, undo, event store with catch-up. Its state is recorded in the web completion notes as it lands.

## Decisions recorded

- `expected_version` is optional on every mutation and enforced when present; clients send it for destructive edits and omit it for appends.
- Open orders are re-priced with the rules active at the time of the change; adjustments keep the rule snapshot.
- Partial voids keep the remainder on the original line and record the voided quantity on a new voided line (zero amounts), so kitchen tickets and audit keep both halves.
- PIN sign-in uses an HMAC lookup column (unique per merchant) plus scrypt verification, so sign-in is one indexed query, and a database leak alone cannot brute-force PINs. Duplicate PINs within a merchant are refused at creation.
- `payments.void` (vision open question 1) is not built; wrong tenders are corrected through reopen or refund in v0.6/v0.7.
